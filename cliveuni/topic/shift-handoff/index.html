The Shift Handoff That Actually Works

The two most common service failures happen at shift transitions: information that was known by the outgoing manager is lost, and problems that started on one shift become invisible on the next. A structured shift handoff prevents both. The outgoing manager writes three things before leaving: what is working, what needs attention, and any guest or staff issues in progress. The incoming manager reads it, asks one clarifying question, and acknowledges. This takes five minutes.

Common restaurant operating practice, referenced in DSP management systems

Key Insights

The handoff is not a novel. It is three short statements written on a consistent template. The intended outcome is fewer dropped reservations, fewer repeated guest issues, and stronger continuity between services. The format matters less than the consistency. Whether it is a notebook, a shared document, or a whiteboard, the discipline is the same: do not leave the building without handing off the shift.
