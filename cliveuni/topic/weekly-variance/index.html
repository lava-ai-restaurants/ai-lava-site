The Weekly Variance Conversation

Most restaurants review food cost monthly, which means problems compound for weeks before anyone sees them. The weekly variance conversation changes this. Every week, the manager pulls beginning inventory, purchases, and ending inventory. They calculate actual food cost percentage and compare it to the budget target. The meeting is not the owner asking what happened.

Adapted from David Scott Peters weekly food cost methodology

Key Insights

The meeting is the manager arriving with three things: the number, the cause, and the fix. When a manager arrives with the actual result, the target, the identified cause, and a par-level correction already underway, the conversation operates at a different level. The owner's role shifts from interrogator to coach. This structure works because it is weekly, because the manager owns the number, and because the corrective action happens before the next period begins. The variance conversation is not a report. It is a discipline.

Knowledge Check

According to "The Weekly Variance Conversation", what is the recommended approach?

  • Calculate actual food cost every week, not monthly
  • Wait until the end of the quarter to review results.
  • Delegate all responsibility without verification.

What is a key takeaway from "The Weekly Variance Conversation"?

  • None of the practices described matter in real operations.
  • The manager presents the number, the cause, and the corrective action
  • Only large restaurant chains need these systems.
