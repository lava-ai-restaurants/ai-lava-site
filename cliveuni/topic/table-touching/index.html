Table Touching Is a Management Skill, Not a Personality Trait

Some managers touch tables naturally. Most do not, and the ones who avoid it often believe it is a personality trait rather than a skill. Table touching is a management skill that can be structured and practiced. The manager's presence on the floor during service serves two purposes: it builds direct relationships with guests, and it provides real-time oversight of service execution. When guests begin asking for a manager by name rather than just the owner, that is a signal that the culture is working.

Adapted from David Scott Peters guest relationship methodology

Key Insights

The practice is simple: during peak service, the manager visits every table in their section. Not to sell, not to apologize, but to connect. A brief check-in, a genuine question about the meal, and an acknowledgment. The discipline is doing it every shift, not just when things go wrong. Managers who practice structured table touching also catch service problems earlier, because they see what is happening at the table level instead of from behind the host stand.
