The Scheduling Trap That Costs You Every Week

Most managers schedule by filling shifts. They look at who is available, plug people in, and publish. The problem is that this approach has no financial target. You discover whether you were over budget after payroll runs, which is too late to fix. Scheduling on budget means starting with a labor cost target for the week, calculated from projected sales.

Based on David Scott Peters Restaurant Payroll Guardian framework

Key Insights

The manager builds the schedule against that target, seeing projected hours and dollars before publishing. If Thursday dinner is projected over its labor target, the adjustment happens before the schedule goes live, not after the payroll report. The shift from reactive to proactive scheduling requires two things: a sales forecast and scheduling software that shows labor dollars alongside hours. Without both, the manager is guessing. With both, the manager is managing. This is the difference between a schedule that fills shifts and a schedule that controls cost.
