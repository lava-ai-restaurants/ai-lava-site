From Vague Accountability to Controlled Shift Handoffs

Cala operating story

10 min3 lessons2 checks

Clive operating example — sanitized

Lessons

Challenge

Shift accountability at Cala was undefined. Food runners started and ended shifts without a structured check-in or check-out. Dry storage lacked consistent signoff controls. The result was gaps in responsibility: tasks were assumed completed without verification, and problems discovered on one shift could not be traced to their origin.

From Vague Accountability to Controlled Shift Handoffs — approved Clive University case-study source

Move

Cala implemented a food-runner check-in and check-out process. Each food runner now confirms readiness at shift start and completes a structured handoff at shift end. Dry-storage signoff controls were introduced so that inventory movements are recorded and attributable. The changes created a clear chain of responsibility across shifts.

From Vague Accountability to Controlled Shift Handoffs — approved Clive University case-study source

Operating Lesson

Accountability requires structure, not just expectation. When check-in, check-out, and signoff controls exist, responsibility becomes traceable. Vague accountability produces vague results.
